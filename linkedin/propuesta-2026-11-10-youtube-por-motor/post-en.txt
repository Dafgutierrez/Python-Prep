“YouTube is the most-cited site in AI search.”

That headline is everywhere this week. It is also an average, and the average hides the story.

Two AI visibility vendors publish the data, and both split it by engine.

RankSpot, 129,537 answers, 1 Sep to 10 Nov 2026: YouTube is cited in 22.5% of Google AI Overviews answers, 10.2% of AI Mode and 0.9% of ChatGPT. The blended figure is 10.8%.

Searchable, 29 Aug to 27 Sep 2026: 31.2% on AI Overviews, 29.5% on AI Mode, and between 0.5% and 2.6% on Perplexity, Gemini, ChatGPT and Copilot.

Different questions and windows, same shape. Both are vendor data on questions their own customers track. They show what engines cited, not market share.

My take: “AI cites YouTube” is the wrong lesson. Every engine has its own source diet, and an average across engines tells you almost nothing about the engine your buyers actually use.

What I would do this week:
1. Add “ChatGPT, Google AI or another AI” to “how did you find us?”
2. Run your 10 buyer questions in each engine and write down who is cited.
3. Then pick the lever. For Google AI, one B2B study found cited videos have buying-question titles and timestamps. For ChatGPT, YouTube appears in 1 to 2% of answers in these studies, so study who it does cite.

Measure per engine. Report per engine. An average is a headline, not a metric.

Which engine do your buyers actually ask?

Sources: RankSpot AI Citation Index (10 Nov 2026), Searchable (Sep 2026 report), Foundation Inc YouTube study. Links in the first comment.
