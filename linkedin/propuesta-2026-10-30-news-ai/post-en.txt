How well do AI chatbots answer questions about news from the same day?

A preprint from Stanford University and Together AI tested six chatbots with web search on. They asked 2,100 multiple-choice questions generated from same-day BBC News articles across six regional services, over 14 days in February 2026.

What they found:
→ The four best chatbots scored above 90% on multiple choice about events reported hours earlier. On a one-day check with open answers, accuracy dropped from 86.9% to about 70 to 71%.
→ Web search mattered. Turning it off raised errors by 31 to 46 points on the US and Canada questions.
→ Most mistakes came from retrieval. Retrieval failure and source divergence made up 71.5% of the 1,497 errors the authors classified. When the right source was retrieved, the model almost always got the answer right.
→ Citing more did not mean answering better. Gemini 3 Pro had the lowest citation rate (84.7%) and 93.7% accuracy. GPT-5 had a 97.8% citation rate and 85.0% accuracy.
→ False premises were the weak spot. When a question hid a wrong assumption, GPT-5 scored 19.0% and accepted false premises 64% of the time. Grok 4 scored 70.0%.

The limits: it is a preprint, not confirmed as peer reviewed. It is a 14-day snapshot. BBC is unusually well indexed, so less prominent sources may do worse. Multiple choice likely inflates accuracy. All questions were generated by one model. And it did not test Spanish or Mexico: the regions were US and Canada in English, Arabic, French, Hindi, Russian and Turkish.

My read (opinion): in this study, most errors came from what the engine retrieved, not from how it phrased the answer. So being retrievable comes first. Google’s documentation says a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet to appear in its AI features. Whether answering in the first lines helps is something this study did not test.

What I would do this week:
1️⃣ Check that my key pages are indexed.
2️⃣ Ask a chatbot with search on my customer’s question and save the answer, its sources and the date.
3️⃣ Ask it again with a wrong assumption inside, such as a feature I do not offer, and see whether it corrects it.
4️⃣ Repeat in a month.

When an AI answers about your business, do you know which sources it used?

Sources: Suzgun et al., “Evaluating Commercial AI Chatbots as News Intermediaries,” arXiv preprint (21 May 2026); Stanford HAI summary (3 June 2026); Google Search documentation on AI features.
