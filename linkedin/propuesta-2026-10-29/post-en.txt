You can rank first on Google and not exist for ChatGPT. Four technical reasons no rankings report will show you.

My take: “does ranking #1 on Google mean ChatGPT will recommend me?” is answered by a technical review, not an opinion. Here is what I would check, with what the official sources say.

1️⃣ Blocking the wrong bot
OpenAI has three crawlers with different jobs: OAI-SearchBot surfaces sites in ChatGPT search, GPTBot is for training models, and ChatGPT-User handles actions a person asks for. Each setting is independent. Blocking GPTBot does not take you out of search; a blanket rule against “AI bots” can.

2️⃣ An open robots.txt is not enough
The file says what you allow, not what your server answers to the bot. A firewall or CDN can reject it anyway, which is why OpenAI recommends also allowing the IP ranges it publishes for OAI-SearchBot. And because a person starts ChatGPT-User visits, robots.txt may not apply to them.

3️⃣ A page that is not eligible
Google requires the page to be indexed and eligible to be shown with a snippet. The controls that limit snippets (nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet) are listed in the same guide. My reading, not Google’s wording: a tag you added for another reason could keep you out of its AI answers.

4️⃣ Mixing up the control
Google-Extended limits AI training and grounding in some of Google’s other systems; the AI features guide does not say it controls AI Overviews. For Search, the crawl control is robots.txt for Googlebot. And there is no schema or special file to add: structured data only has to match the visible text.

What is not known: how much of your traffic comes from each one. Google counts traffic from its AI features inside the Performance report (Web search type), not in a separate one; ChatGPT tags its visits with utm_source=chatgpt.com.

I used to think “optimizing for AI” was its own discipline. Now I think much of it is checking what you already control and are not looking at.

Which of the four had you never checked?

Sources: Google Search Central (AI features in Search, read 28 October 2026); OpenAI bots documentation and its publishers FAQ.
